Short answer: cooked chicken lasts 3 to 4 days in the refrigerator. If you will not eat it by then, freeze it. Below is how to store, freeze, thaw and reheat it safely, based on USDA guidance.

#### Fridge (40 F C or below): 3 to 4 days. Freezer (0 F / -18 C or below): 3 to 4 months for best quality. Frozen food stays safe indefinitely but can lose moisture and flavor over time. Source: USDA FSIS, Leftovers and Food Safety. The FoodSafety.gov chart lists the same 3 to 4 days for cooked leftovers in the fridge.

Refrigerate within 2 hours: Put cooked chicken in the fridge within 2 hours of cooking, or within 1 hour if it is hotter than 90 F outside. Throw away perishable food left out longer than that.

Cool it fast: Slice large pieces and divide chicken and sauces into shallow containers so they chill quickly. Cover or seal in airtight packaging.

How to thaw: Safe methods per USDA are in the refrigerator (slowest, stays safe the whole time), in cold water in a leak-proof bag (cook before refreezing), or in the microwave. Chicken thawed in the fridge should be used within 3 to 4 days or can be refrozen.

How to reheat without drying it out: Reheat to 165 F, measured with a food thermometer, and check several spots in the microwave. Cover the dish to keep moisture in, and add a splash of broth or water if needed. Bring soups, sauces and gravies to a rolling boil.

Can you eat cold leftover chicken? It is safe if it was stored properly and is within the 3 to 4 day window. The 165 F target applies to reheating.
